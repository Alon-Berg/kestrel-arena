# Kestrel Arena

A desktop app that runs chess-engine tournaments with real games between UCI engines, and lets you play against and analyse with the **Kestrel** engine that is bundled with it. Every result comes from an actual game - nothing is estimated or drawn with random numbers.

Start it with `KestrelArena.exe` (or `python main.py` from the source folder). Nothing is installed and no network is used: the app talks to the engines through UCI and shows its pages in a small local window.

## Tournament tab - real games
* Pick engines (quick picks: "Kestrel + 7 nearest" in Elo, top 8, top 16), a format (**round robin, gauntlet, Swiss, knockout**), the number of games per pairing and a time control (clock + increment, fixed time, nodes or depth per move).
* Games run in parallel (set "Parallel games"). Every pair of games uses the same opening in both colours; openings come from the bundled balanced suite (600 positions made with Kestrel), your own EPD/PGN file, or the start position.
* Live view: boards with ticking clocks and evaluations, standings with a fitted **Elo +- error**, W/D/L, score and Sonneborn-Berger, a crosstable, a bracket (knockout), a replay viewer with move list and evaluation graph, PGN and CSV export.
* **Pause**, **Stop** and **Continue**: a stopped tournament (even after closing the app) is listed under "Saved tournaments" and continues with the games that are missing. Results are in `data/tournaments/<id>/`.
* Games are played to their natural end (mate, draw by rule) - adjudication is off by default; you can switch on draw / win adjudication (shown in the setup) to save time. Illegal moves, crashes and flag falls lose the game; the time margin (default 600 ms, the value your fastchess runs use) is the allowed overrun.

**Very fast games.** Many engines keep a safety margin per move for themselves (`Move Overhead`, `TimeMargin` ...; 50 to 250 ms is common), which is a large part of a game of a second or two. Some then play badly or answer with an invalid move when the clock gets low: in a round robin at 0.5 s + 0.005 s per move 15 of 101 engines lost 6 % or more of their games (11 of them more than a tenth) to time forfeits, "illegal move" and "no move" (part of it while the PC was busy with other jobs). The setting **Engine time overhead** (default: automatic) turns the margin down to 5 ms for engines that have such an option when the clock is 3 s or less; you can also pick a value or leave the engines alone. Six engines of the roster still could not play such games however their margin was set, so the Arena has **built-in workarounds** for them (`KNOWN_QUIRKS` in `arena/engines.py`; they act in tournaments and in `check_roster.py`, never in the Play tab): *Bagatur* (Java) rebuilds everything for a second whenever a game starts from another position and is slow while its JIT is cold, so it gets a 2 s warm-up search when it starts and a depth-1 search of each game's start position before the clocks run; *Quanticade* has a timer that ticks every 15.6 ms and sometimes answers "no move" to a very short search; *Serendipity*, *Smallbrain*, *Altair* and *Rice* then answer with their previous (illegal) move, *Uralochka* with the null move - so in very fast games **every engine gets a second chance**: an answer that is no move or an illegal move is asked for again, up to twice (20 ms or more, then 100 ms or more), and the failed tries do not count on the engine's clock (`"quirks": {"retry_bad_move": false}` in an engine's entry switches it off; longer games get no retry); *Yukari* (crashes below a 30 ms movetime, takes 19 ms off every movetime), *Serendipity* (pauses of 10 to 30 ms, so it needs 30 ms per move: at 0.5 s + 0.005 s it plays with about twice the time of the others) and *Smallbrain* (overshoots a nearly empty clock) get a time per move computed from the clock (`go movetime`, a fortieth of the clock plus most of the increment) when the clock is 3 s or less. Windows' automatic efficiency throttling (EcoQoS) is switched off for every engine the Arena starts and for the Arena itself: with it on, Serendipity needed more than 25 ms for its first answer in one search of ten and more than 178 ms in one of a hundred (3 % of its searches were answered without searching at all); with it off the same figures are 8 ms and 47 ms. Result at 0.5 s + 0.005 s: all 100 engines of the roster played 3,600 games (12 and 24 per engine, two runs with different openings) against Kestrel without a single fault; two later runs of 1,200 games each (the PC had been under test load for hours) lost 8 and 4 games to a fault, by different engines each time (the 8 were Altair's and Rice's, before every engine got the second try), so expect about 1 game in 100 for a few engines at the margin. A round robin of the six engines with workarounds and Kestrel (5 games in parallel, the default on a 22-thread PC) lost about 0.2 to 0.5 % of its games to a fault (nearly all Serendipity's, in the first seconds of a tournament), with 8 games in parallel about 1 in 100, and with 11 (every logical CPU busy) about 1 in 25 - keep the number of parallel games moderate for such short games. Titan is the exception the other way round: it ignores `go movetime`, so use a clock, not a fixed time per move, with it. Elixir takes 100 ms off every `go movetime` on its own (`DEFAULT_MOVE_OVERHEAD` in its source, so `go movetime 100` is a depth-1 search), so for a fixed time per move the Arena adds 95 ms to it (`movetime_add`; 12 games at 100 ms per move: no fault and games of normal length, against 3 illegal moves and 11 lost games after 12 to 51 moves without). The table is only used for the roster ids; an engine entry of `settings.json` with its own `"quirks"` object (even an empty one) replaces it. An engine can still be unfit for a time control: `python tools/check_roster.py --tc 0.5+0.005` plays every engine of the list against Kestrel and reports start-up times, faults and second tries before you start a long tournament. Promotions written in capital letters (`a7a8Q`) are accepted.

**Patched engine builds.** For three of those engines the bug behind the faults was found in the engine's open source and fixed there (Quanticade Cronus 3.0, Yukari 2025.11.1, Smallbrain 7; details, patches and build commands in `tools/engine_patches/`). If `engines/patched/<id>.exe` exists, a tournament plays it instead of the official exe, shows the engine as `... (patched)` and does not use the workarounds above for it; `python tools/check_roster.py --official` and `"patched": false` in an engine's entry of `data/settings.json` switch that off. The search is the same (equal node counts at a fixed depth), only the broken time handling changed. With every workaround of the Arena switched off, the official exes lost 8 of 48 games (Quanticade, 0.5 s + 0.005 s, "no move"), 4 of 48 (Yukari, same, crash) and 25 of 48 (Smallbrain, 0.25 s + 0.0025 s, time forfeit); the patched builds lost none in 48 games each. The builds themselves are not in the repository (`engines/` is ignored): rebuild them as described there.

For fair results keep *parallel games x threads* below the number of logical CPUs. Engines and the app run at **above-normal priority** (setup: *Process priority*), so programs that work in the background cannot make a move late: with the PC busy (a Stockfish labelling job on every core) the same 30-game test lost 18 games on time at normal priority and none at above normal. On a quiet PC the setting makes no difference. At very fast time controls (3+0.03) a flag is still possible when the CPU is overloaded by the games themselves.

## Play tab
Play against Kestrel (or any engine in your list) by clicking a piece and its target square. Kestrel plays at **full strength** and you choose the thinking time (time, nodes or depth per move), as on the web page (https://alon-berg.github.io/Kestrel-chess-engine/play/). The twelve weaker levels that the Play tab offered for Kestrel in the first versions were removed, as they were on the page. Kestrel's own `UCI_Elo` option is not offered either: it limits the nodes searched per move, but the engine looks at its node count only every 2,048 nodes, so every setting below about 2300 plays alike. For other engines that have a `UCI_Elo` option the strength can still be limited with it. *Take back*, *Resign*, PGN download. The **Analysis** board shows the engine's best lines (1-5) for any position; move pieces for both sides, paste a FEN.

## Engines tab
Kestrel 3.2 is bundled (`engines/Kestrel`; the build for your CPU - AVX2, SSE4.2 or generic - is chosen automatically, and a newer `kestrel-<version>-avx2.exe` dropped into that folder shows up by itself). Add any UCI engine (file, optional command-line arguments, Elo, UCI options). "Import tournament roster" makes the list match the roster of the `chess_engine_tournament_roundrobin` project (the newest `config/verify_results_updated_*.json`, else `config/verify_results.json`): new engines are added with their ratings, engines whose file or version changed are updated (options you set stay), engines the roster no longer has are switched off (not deleted), and engines you added yourself are never touched. Engines start with a generous time limit (60 s), because some need more than 20 s to load their network on a busy PC.

## Running from source
Windows 10/11 with Python 3.12 (Edge WebView2 is part of Windows 11).
1. `pip install -r requirements.txt`
2. Put the Kestrel builds into `engines/Kestrel/` - the engine files are not stored in the repository, see `engines/README.md` (the packaged exe contains them). Without them the app still works with engines you add yourself.
3. `python main.py` (or `python main.py --browser` to use your web browser instead of the app window).
4. Optional: `python tools/build_exe.py` builds `dist/KestrelArena/KestrelArena.exe` (needs PyInstaller and Pillow; it keeps the `data` folder of an earlier build); `python tools/make_openings.py` rebuilds the opening suite with Kestrel.

## Files
* `data/settings.json` - engine list and preferences; `data/tournaments/` - saved tournaments (delete a folder to remove one). Both are created when you use the app and are not part of the repository.
* Source: `arena/` (Python: UCI, games, formats, tournaments, levels, server), `web/` (the interface), `tests/` (`python tests/smoke_backend.py`, `smoke_resume.py`, `test_formats.py`, `test_levels.py`, `test_roster_sync.py`, `test_overhead.py`, `test_quirks.py`, `test_patched.py`), `tools/` (`build_exe.py`, `check_roster.py`, `engine_patches/` - patches and notes for patched engine builds, `make_openings.py`, `make_roster_snapshot.py`, `make_logo.py`, `make_level_vectors.py`).
* `arena/levels.py` (a port of the twelve human-like levels in `docs/play/levels.js` of the Kestrel repository) and its test `tests/test_levels.py` are still in the source, but the Play tab no longer offers levels. To keep the port in step with the page: `python tools/make_level_vectors.py` (needs Deno) rewrites `tests/levels_vectors.json` from the JavaScript; update `TABLE` in `arena/levels.py` until `python tests/test_levels.py` passes.
* `assets/logo/` - the Kestrel logo (a white K on a small chessboard; SVG, PNG 16-1024 px, ICO), the same one for the engine and the app; `python tools/make_logo.py` redraws it.
* `data/openings.epd` (600 balanced opening positions made with Kestrel) and `data/roster_ratings.json` (ratings of the 100-engine roster tournament, used when importing the roster) are shipped with the app.
